The live pilot additionally runs with the assistant turned off. That affects finding 1.

**Verdict:** the visual craft is solid: calm, consistent and readable. But the experience doesn't yet express what Relay *is*, a place where buyers trust that work gets done. Trust is mostly invisible, the "conversation" doesn't talk back on the pilot, and the product speaks its internal vocabulary.

## What works

- **A consistent visual system.** The dark theme, type scale, spacing and button hierarchy all hold together, and nothing looks broken or templated.
- **One clear next step per state.** A draft shows "Review task request", offers show "Continue with this agent", and work in progress shows "Pause work".
- **A recommendation, not a wall of offers.** Alternatives sit behind a "See 1 alternative offer" disclosure, which is right for most buyers.
- **Honest guardrails.** The copy for Pause, Cancel and requesting an agent explains the consequences, and "No verified runs yet" is shown rather than hidden.
- **A real progress timeline and Activity log.** The timeline reads Authorized → Started → Delivery pending → Your review, and the Activity log is readable and clearly attributed (you / agent).
- **A builder dashboard that's genuinely useful.** It has counts, a "Needs attention" list, active work and recent offers.

## Findings, ranked

1. **The conversation doesn't converse (pilot).** With the assistant off, the buyer types, nothing replies, and the page says "When you're ready, review what you'd like done". The chat-first promise turns into a form with a chat skin. This is the single biggest gap between product and promise. *Fix:* either turn on the hosted assistant (Decisions §4), or, until then, have the composer lead into a guided brief with outcome, deliverable, deadline, budget and acceptance criteria. Don't present an empty chat as the experience.
2. **The moat is invisible where people decide.**
   - The agent grid shows "1 qualified" and tags, but no track record.
   - The offer card's evidence reads like system logs: "assessment evidence is available, but verified runs are not established" and "balanced across 2 eligible USD quotes".
   - *Fix:* show one plain evidence line everywhere an agent appears (e.g. "Passed Relay's CSV test · 0 paid runs yet"), and a "Why this one" with three short, human reasons.
3. **Internal vocabulary leaks.** Examples: "Recommended execution", "execution option", "Cancel execution", "The supplier started work", "Capability matches your request: normalize, csv, data, spreadsheet" (a keyword dump), "Request builder" vs "Request agent". *Fix:* a buyer vocabulary pass: *agent*, *work*, *offer*, *stop*, *why it fits*.
4. **Status logic tells the buyer the wrong thing.**
   - Work shows **Needs you 0** while a task has offers waiting for the buyer's choice. Offers ready should count as "Needs you".
   - **All 2** leaves out the draft, which the sidebar lists. "All" has to mean all.
5. **The landing page sells the wrong product.**
   - "Find the right AI agent" positions Relay as a directory, while the thesis is handled, verified outcomes.
   - 6 of 8 integration tiles say "Planned", which reads as vapourware.
   - The mascots undercut trust for larger projects. The "projects at all scale" positioning needs at least one example of a larger engagement.
   - The page is long: about 9 sections including the FAQ and the closing call to action.
   - *Fix:* lead with outcomes and the guarantee (agreed criteria, pay on acceptance, try another agent), show only the integrations that work, and cut to about 5 sections.
6. **No linkable pages.** You can't link to agent profiles or other app pages, because `#agent=…` and `#agents` don't route. That blocks sharing and the public hub. *Fix:* real routes for agent profiles and tasks.
7. **The sidebar is redundant.** "New chat", "Work" and "Recent conversations" overlap, and "Workspace" is a bare disclosure triangle. *Fix:* use New request, Work (with recents inside), Agents and Activity, and move the rest into a settings menu.
8. **Agent cards have too many actions.** Each card carries 4 actions (Save, Request builder, Request agent, View profile). *Fix:* make the card open the profile and keep one primary action.
9. **Visual defects.**
   - The notification badge renders as a white blob: the count is there (7), but unreadable.
   - The "In progress" card's shimmer washes out its own title.
   - On mobile, the docked composer sits over the offer content. Needs a check on a real device.
10. **Not built for scale yet.** One offer card and a single price fit a $5 job. A larger project needs:
    - a proposal (approach, milestones, assumptions);
    - side-by-side comparison;
    - acceptance criteria per milestone.

    That's the engagements spec plus the acceptance work in progress.

## Suggested order

- **Quick (hours):** findings 4, 9 and 3.
- **Superseded by the marketplace build:** 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8.
- **With the larger features:** proposals, comparison and milestones (10), together with acceptance criteria.
