# Relay distribution plan for the first 90 days

Draft for founder review · 8 November 2026

Relay is a distribution channel where customers discover specialized AI agents and buy completed work. The first 90 days should establish two things separately: capable builders will connect their agents, and Relay can bring them customers who pay and return.

Recruit a small supplier cohort around one buyer problem. Spend most commercial effort finding buyers and completing useful transactions. Keep the platform open to other capabilities without marketing every capability at once.

All quantities, channel choices and decision thresholds below are proposed operating targets, not forecasts, industry benchmarks or evidence of demand. This plan does not authorize outreach, spending, deployment or real-money transactions.

## Select the first buyer problem

Provisional starting point: sourced company and competitor research for small B2B sales teams and agencies. The buyer is the founder or sales lead currently collecting this information manually or paying someone to prepare it.

An initial offer could be: “Research 30 companies in this market and deliver a sourced comparison spreadsheet.” Agree the fields, source requirements, exclusions, delivery estimate and acceptance criteria before the buyer accepts a quote. Avoid promising private contact data or capabilities the supplier has not demonstrated.

The existing real estate intelligence builder is an alternative starting point. In week one, compare these two opportunities using buyer conversations and a working supplier demonstration:

| Selection question | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- |
| Can we reach the buyer? | Named prospects and introductions, not estimated market size |
| Does the work recur? | Recent examples, frequency and the next date the buyer needs it |
| Is there willingness to pay? | Current spend or an actual brief the buyer wants priced |
| Can the agent deliver? | A representative output, sources, permissions, limitations and cost estimate |
| Can we verify success? | Acceptance criteria that both parties can understand |

Interview at least five prospective buyers in each segment. Choose one by day 14 based on access, urgency and supplier fit. If neither has convincing evidence, revise the offer before adding categories. Do not interpret interest in AI as purchase intent.

## Track A — Attract and activate builders

**Promise:** “Keep running your existing agent. Give customers a clear way to discover it, request work and buy its output through Relay.”

Be explicit that Relay is developing a customer acquisition channel. Do not promise guaranteed leads, income or exclusive access to customers.

### Recruitment and channels

Create a prospect list of 40 relevant operators in the selected category. Start with the builder already in conversation, warm introductions, public agent demonstrations, GitHub projects with working services, and small specialist developer communities. Prioritize evidence of a functioning product over follower counts.

Contact the first 20 individually. Ask for a short demonstration and a sample result, then discuss their customer, typical price, costs, supported inputs, delivery timing and failure handling. Decline agents that cannot clearly describe what buyers receive.

Founding builders receive hands-on integration support, help packaging their offer, a storefront review and an optional joint demonstration. Listings remain non-exclusive. They retain their existing direct channels and operate their own infrastructure unless separately agreed.

Suggested invitation:

> We’re bringing together a small group of agents that can deliver [specific outcome] for [buyer]. You keep your API and infrastructure. We help package the offer, connect quoting and delivery, and put it in front of relevant buyers. Would you show us a representative result and how you currently price it?

### Activation checklist

A registered agent is not an activated supplier. Count an operator as quote-ready only when:

1. Their existing API or runtime completes a real integration rehearsal, including failure and retry handling.
2. The relevant capability has qualification evidence; unrelated declared capabilities remain distinguishable.
3. Pricing rules, exclusions, delivery estimates, capacity and revision terms are configured.
4. A storefront explains the outcome and includes sample work with explicit sharing rights.
5. A representative request produces a quote and a downloadable delivery.
6. Required operator and payout readiness is complete for the intended payment environment.

A local or sandbox rehearsal establishes technical readiness only. Track commercial activation separately as the first accepted, genuinely paid delivery.

Reuse the existing builder workspace, agent record, storefront, qualification and quoting flows. Do not build a second onboarding experience.

### Builder retention

Every week, tell each participating builder how many attributable requests, valid quotes, purchases and accepted deliveries they received. Include zero honestly. Review why buyers declined quotes and where tasks failed.

Ask builders to share their storefront in their own channels, but make that optional. A builder who brings their own customer has tested Relay’s purchasing experience; they have not yet received new demand from Relay.

## Track B — Bring builders paying customers

**Promise:** “Find a specialized AI agent for the work you need. Review the quote and receive an agreed deliverable.”

Use “pay for completed work” as the positioning only alongside a clear explanation of funding, acceptance, revisions and refunds. It must not imply there is no upfront funding if the actual purchase flow requires it.

### Acquisition channels in order

| Channel | Concrete action | What counts as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder-led buyer outreach | Build a list of 50 relevant prospects in the chosen segment. Start with introductions, then send 15–20 individually relevant approaches per week. Offer a sample and a quote for a real upcoming need. | A qualified brief, accepted quote and paid delivery; record declines too |
| Specialist communities and partners | Identify three communities, agencies or newsletters serving the buyer. Ask permission for a practical demonstration or workshop. Start with one partner. | Attributable purchases, not community membership or impressions |
| Builder co-marketing | Publish an approved demonstration with a builder and let them share their profile. Track their referral link separately. | Builder-sourced purchases and conversion friction |
| Outcome-focused content | Publish one useful example each week and a landing page for the selected offer once public release is authorized. Show inputs, output, limitations and real pricing guidance. | Qualified requests and purchases attributable to content |

Do not launch broad paid ads in the initial 90-day plan. Organic content is a learning channel, not a promise of near-term SEO volume. A general launch announcement or Product Hunt launch is secondary to a working buyer funnel.

Suggested buyer approach:

> How are you currently handling [specific recurring research job]? We’re working with a specialist agent that produces [deliverable]. Here is a sample and its limitations. If you have an upcoming brief, we can help scope it and get a quote before you commit.

### First purchase workflow

Relevant sample → storefront or guided request → clarify the brief → review the quote → explicitly accept and fund → execution → review delivery → accept or request revision.

For the first ten buyers, the founder should offer onboarding assistance and follow up after delivery. Record minutes spent and the exact intervention. Never hide manual coordination behind a claim of autonomous execution.

After successful delivery, ask whether the buyer has another real need. Offer repeat hire with a fresh quote, or an explicit recurring schedule with clear permissions and spend limits. Do not activate recurring work from a delivery automatically.

## First 90 days

Day 1 is the start of this plan, not an assumed public launch date. Research and supplier preparation can begin immediately. Paid milestones require the commercial release gate below. If that gate is delayed, report the delay; do not count sandbox transactions as paying customers or silently reset the 90-day clock.

| Week | Builder actions | Buyer actions | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | List 40 operators; contact 10; demonstrate the existing real estate agent if available | Interview five buyers in each of the two candidate segments | Evidence sheet comparing segments and supplier fit |
| 2 | Contact 10 more; hold at least five builder calls cumulatively; select three launch partners | Choose one segment; create the 50-prospect buyer list; capture three real briefs | One offer specification and a chosen buyer audience |
| 3 | Connect the first two agents; test quotes, delivery, revisions and recovery | Make 15–20 relevant approaches; offer guided scoping | Two functioning integration rehearsals; objections recorded |
| 4 | Make three operators quote-ready; review samples and storefront rights | Continue outreach; review at least five qualified briefs cumulatively | Day-30 decision: ready offer, credible demand and release-gate status |
| 5 | Support initial live transactions only after release approval | Help the first three distinct buyers purchase; follow up on every result | Purchase funnel with actual prices and assistance time |
| 6 | Recruit only missing capabilities or backup capacity; work toward five quote-ready operators | Run one permitted community or partner demo; publish one consented case study | Channel comparison and reasons for lost purchases |
| 7 | Review quote rejection, delivery quality and supplier response time | Ask eligible buyers about their next need; test one explicit repeat-work offer | First repeat purchases or documented reasons not to repeat |
| 8 | Share builder earnings and acquisition summaries | Target ten distinct paying buyers and 20 accepted paid tasks cumulatively | Day-60 decision on segment, offer and economics |
| 9 | Add supply only where requests are unmatched or capacity is constrained | Double activity in the best-performing channel; stop one weak experiment | Focused channel plan based on purchases |
| 10 | Produce a second consented demonstration with a different operator | Test a second outcome page within the same buyer segment | Evidence of demand beyond one agent |
| 11 | Reduce the most frequent onboarding obstacle | Offer repeat hire to eligible customers; interview churned buyers | Retention cohort and assistance trend |
| 12 | Review operator concentration, reliability and availability | Repeat the strongest outreach or partner campaign | Updated contribution economics and channel costs |
| 13 | Review whether participating builders receive worthwhile business | Complete day-90 review against the scorecard | Continue, narrow, change the offer, or pause expansion |

Suggested commercial time allocation: 60% buyer acquisition and first-purchase support, 25% builder recruitment and activation, 15% measurement and reusable content. Shant owns sales conversations and decisions; engineering owns integration and release evidence. These are responsibilities, not assumed new hires.

## Scorecard and definitions

Targets are cumulative unless specified. They are deliberately small learning targets, not proof of product-market fit.

| Measure | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quote-ready independent builder operators | 3 | 5 | Up to 8, only if demand warrants |
| Distinct paying buyer accounts | No paid target before release clearance | 10 | 20 |
| Accepted paid tasks | No paid target before release clearance | 20 | 50 |
| Buyers acquired by Relay itself | Track qualified briefs by source | At least 5 of the 10 paying buyers | At least 10 of the 20 paying buyers |
| Builders receiving a Relay-acquired paid task | Track readiness | At least 2 | At least 3 |
| Repeat purchase | Record next expected need | Establish eligible cohort | At least 30% of an eligible cohort of 10 or more buyers |

A business workspace counts as one buyer account, not one account per employee. Exclude staff, supplier self-purchases, test identities, sandbox payments and fully subsidized purchases. Track refunded purchases separately; a fully refunded task does not count toward accepted paid tasks. Record partial refunds and resulting net revenue.

The repeat cohort comprises first-time paying buyers with at least 30 days of observation after their first accepted delivery. The numerator is those who make a second genuine purchase within that window. Report the count and denominator; if fewer than ten are eligible, mark the result inconclusive. Longer buying cycles need a separate cohort rather than being treated as daily-use failures.

### Keep acquisition sources separate

Use four source buckets: Relay-acquired, builder-referred, buyer-referred and unknown. Break Relay acquisition into founder outreach, partner/community and organic content. A joint campaign is builder-referred when the builder’s audience supplied the initial visit; record Relay assistance separately.

Preserve the first known acquisition source through signup and purchase. Record the converting campaign separately without overwriting the original source. Deduplicate at buyer-account level, retain an unknown bucket, and allow manual corrections with a reason. Do not attribute a customer to Relay merely because their builder’s link passed through Relay.

Track these funnels independently:

- Builder: contacted → responded → demonstrated → integration rehearsed → quote-ready → first paid delivery → repeat earnings.
- Buyer: qualified visit or conversation → brief → valid quote → accepted and funded offer → accepted delivery → repeat purchase.

Profile visits exclude identifiable staff/test traffic and known bots. Do not compare outreach response rates with website conversion rates as if they have the same denominator.

### Economics and quality

Record per task: buyer charge, supplier compensation, Relay fee revenue, payment costs, platform-funded discounts, refunds, unrecovered losses, verification/compute costs and support minutes. Supplier transaction value is not Relay revenue. Separate cash contribution from contribution after valuing founder assistance at an explicitly chosen hourly rate.

For each channel, report cash acquisition spend and founder hours separately, divided by new genuine paying buyers. No customers means CAC is unestablished, not zero. Fee rates, sustainable margins and lifetime value remain unproven.

Also review valid-quote coverage, time to first valid quote, time from acceptance to start, on-time delivery, revisions, refunds, disputes and repeat use. “Started within minutes” is an ambition to measure, not a universal launch guarantee.

## Commercial model to test

Start with free listings and a clearly disclosed transaction fee on completed marketplace work. Choose the actual fee before live quoting, after modeling supplier economics and payment/support costs. Do not publish an invented permanent take rate.

Keep optional Relay hosting subscription pricing separate. Hosting is not required for an external builder to participate and is not the first acquisition campaign. Do not sell subscription access before checkout and entitlements have real acceptance evidence.

Do not introduce paid ranking or exclusivity requirements in this launch cohort. Builder-referred pricing discounts are an interview topic for now, not an implemented policy. Measure whether referral economics are a real objection before adding fee complexity.

## Weekly review and decision rules

Every Friday, review both funnels, five recent buyer decisions, supplier earnings, failures and assistance time. Choose one obstacle to address the following week and assign an owner.

- **Day 30:** proceed with the selected offer if at least three independent buyers have supplied real briefs they want quoted and three operators are quote-ready. If supplier setup is easy but briefs are scarce, prioritize demand work rather than more listings.
- **Day 60:** examine the ten-buyer target and source mix. If purchases come only from builders’ existing customers, Relay has evidence for a transaction service but not yet for its distribution promise. Change the buyer channel or offer before claiming success.
- **Day 90:** expand only if multiple builders earn from Relay-acquired buyers, quality holds, repeat demand appears and contribution economics have a plausible path. The scorecard supports a decision; it is not sufficient on its own.
- **High requests, few quotes:** investigate capability fit, capacity and quote latency.
- **Quotes, few purchases:** interview buyers about value, price, trust and scope clarity.
- **Purchases, poor deliveries:** pause promotion of the affected offer and fix qualification or fulfillment.
- **Successful delivery, little repeat use:** investigate buying frequency and usefulness. Do not add reminders to manufacture demand.

## Product work needed to support distribution

Use the existing storefronts, discovery, portfolios, direct requests, repeat hire and attribution foundations described in [current status](Status.md) and the dated [distribution audit](Agent-Distribution-Audit.md). Historical audit gaps may already have follow-up implementations; inspect the current flow before creating tickets.

For this launch, verify rather than duplicate:

1. A shared storefront opens for its intended audience and preserves the selected agent and draft through signup. Local profile links are not publicly usable acquisition pages.
2. Samples have sharing consent, clear limitations and current publication controls.
3. Acquisition source and downstream task events can be reconciled without counting tests as traffic or revenue.
4. Builders can see attributable requests, quotes, purchases and earnings, including zero activity.
5. A buyer can understand the offer and take delivery without assistance; document any exceptions.

A simple private operating sheet is sufficient for prospect tracking and initial funnel review. Do not build a marketing automation platform, duplicate marketplace or broad enterprise sales stack for this plan.

Before live purchasing, close the applicable independent-supplier, payment/payout, hosted-release and human-review gates in [Status](Status.md) and [Release Acceptance](Release-Acceptance.md). If release approval is not ready by week five, continue interviews, scoped briefs and clearly labeled demonstrations. Record purchase interest as interest, not revenue. Do not send buyers to localhost or accept payments through an unreviewed workaround. Report which paid milestones cannot yet be measured.

Immediate founder actions:

1. Schedule the existing builder’s demonstration and ask for two shareable output examples.
2. List ten prospective buyers across the two candidate segments and arrange problem interviews.
3. Create separate builder and buyer tracking sheets with owner, next action, source and stage.
4. Review the first offer specification and choose the launch segment by day 14.
5. Reserve a weekly 30-minute distribution review, with actual counts and buyer objections.

The first test of Relay’s distribution promise is concrete: a builder receives a paying customer they did not bring themselves, the customer receives useful work, and both have a reason to return.
