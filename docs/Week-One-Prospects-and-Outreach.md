# Relay week one prospects and outreach

Prepared 8 November 2026 · Drafts only · Owner: Shant

The active outreach scope has broadened to sales research, creative production and web/data work. Start with the [broader category shortlist](Distribution-Prospects-Broader-Categories.md). This earlier shortlist remains reference material; real estate is an optional warm opportunity rather than the central launch focus.

Start with two separate conversations: builders who can supply a research outcome, and buyers who have a recent research job they might pay someone to complete. The objective is a real brief matched to a capable supplier, not a large number of agent listings.

This initial shortlist contains five prospective suppliers, one data-provider reserve, and ten prospective buyers. None has been contacted or expressed interest through this research. Public product descriptions establish relevance, not independently verified performance. Roles below are suggested recipients; no individual email addresses have been guessed.

Use alongside the [90-day distribution plan](Distribution-Plan.md). All outreach remains unsent. Localhost, Stripe, demo and staging are unchanged; interviews do not require enabling real payments.

## Interview and qualification checklist

Ask every buyer the same core questions before showing a product demo:

1. What was the last company/property research job you actually completed? When?
2. Who requested it, who did it, and what did the finished output look like?
3. How long did it take and what did you pay, if anything? Keep estimates separate from actual spend.
4. What information was missing or unreliable? What would cause you to reject the result?
5. When will you need similar work again? Who owns the budget?
6. Would you provide a non-confidential brief for a supplier to price? What price would require approval?

For each supplier, establish supported geography and inputs, output format and citations, API versus manual operations, turnaround, quote rules and minimum price, revision limits, failure/refund expectations, data licensing, sample-sharing rights, customer-data handling and available capacity. Ask what Relay must contribute beyond their existing API and billing: a new buyer, repeat orders, clearer qualification or less sales effort.

Proposed example briefs, to adapt to actual buyer needs:

- **Company:** research 30 buyer-specified companies; return agreed business fields, evidence of fit, source URLs, retrieval dates and explicit unknowns. No email sending or unsupported private contact data.
- **Property:** research one property or a small buyer-specified set in a supported territory; return agreed market/property facts, source links, dates and gaps. Only include comparable transactions where the supplier has permitted, verifiable access. Do not invent a valuation.

Only request permissioned, non-confidential examples initially. A completed sandbox demonstration is technical evidence, not a paid transaction. A buyer willing to receive a quote is stronger evidence than positive feedback but weaker than a real purchase.

Keep supplier acquisition separate from demand. A supplier's existing customer referred into Relay is builder-sourced, not a Relay-acquired buyer. Record actual provenance rather than assigning every order to the marketplace.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Contacted / replied / interviewed | Pending | Pending |
| Recent concrete research jobs described | Pending | Pending |
| Buyers providing a brief and requesting a quote | Pending | Pending |
| Repeat need and next required date | Pending | Pending |
| Current spend and budget owner confirmed | Pending | Pending |
| Supplier can fulfill the same geography and scope | Pending | Pending |
| Sample meets buyer's acceptance criteria | Pending | Pending |
| Price minus supplier, platform and assistance costs | Unknown | Unknown |

Do not choose a winner from response rate alone. Prefer the segment where several buyers share a recurring problem, a supplier can fulfill it with evidence, and an actual brief can be quoted economically. Treat the geography and firm-size differences in this shortlist as confounders; the first interviews guide recruitment, not a statistically valid market comparison.

For each prospect, record: contact date, route/recipient, source link, reply, interview date, recent job, current spend, recurrence, budget owner, brief permission, quote request, supplier coverage, next action and source attribution. Initial status for every new prospect is **researched — unsent**. The existing builder remains **warm relationship — follow-up not sent**.
